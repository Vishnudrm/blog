Broken window

Nov 7, 2026 1 min read Essay

The window beside me was wide open, waiting for that soothing breeze to kiss the wood that was still burning from the scorching summer heat. It kept hoping that there would be some relief when that cold breeze whispered its revels and gave a fervent hug through the tiny brown chinks. More than that, the timeworn timber hoped to be the shadow of that same playful gale, which never left a trace.

The tales, the joy, and the lightness that the gale brought with it were enough for the hoary timber to cherish. The glaze of the carols the zephyr sang through the fractured pane’s gaze filled the measure of the long dawn. The timber awoke to the eternal silence of anchoring in the hope of becoming the shadow of something that never stops.

In the twilight of the wait, when the gale embraced the languishing heartwood, which was anchored in time, the timber just let out a sigh of relief, drifted into the quiet shores, and settled within the sphere of jagged shards it always carried. It was never about the untouched heavy hours, but about the shattered silence that disturbed me.
